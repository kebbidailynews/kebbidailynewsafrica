content: >-
  Residents across Kebbi State are looking forward to the return of stable
  electricity supply by the end of the week following an official briefing by
  the Managing Director of Kaduna Electric Distribution Company, Dr. Umar
  Abubakar-Hashidu. Speaking at the Government House in Birnin Kebbi, the KEDCO
  chief confirmed that extensive repair work on the damaged 330 kilovolt
  transmission line has reached an advanced stage and that normal service is
  expected to resume in Birnin Kebbi, Argungu and Yauri within days.


  The prolonged outage began weeks earlier when three major transmission towers on the Kainji to Birnin Kebbi line collapsed. The failure disrupted power flow not only within Kebbi but also affected neighbouring areas and parts of the national grid that depend on the same corridor. Low voltage and intermittent supply became a daily reality for households, hospitals, schools, markets and small businesses. Many residents resorted to generators, solar kits or simply went without power for long stretches, increasing the cost of living and slowing economic activity.


  Dr. Abubakar-Hashidu explained that the restoration effort has involved close collaboration between the Transmission Company of Nigeria, Mainstream Energy Solutions and KEDCO technical teams. Engineers have been working around the clock to replace the collapsed towers, restring conductors and test the line under controlled conditions. Four transmission stations along the corridor are expected to return to full function once the final checks are completed. The Managing Director stated that the goal is to end the low voltage challenges that have persisted since the towers fell and to restore reliable energy to the affected communities.


  The announcement has been received with cautious optimism by residents who have endured weeks of hardship. In Birnin Kebbi, shop owners in the central market reported that generator fuel costs had risen sharply, forcing many small traders to reduce operating hours. Cold stores and pharmacies struggled to keep medicines and perishable goods at safe temperatures. Hospitals and health centres relied heavily on backup power, adding pressure to already stretched budgets. In Argungu and Yauri the situation was similar, with farmers and processors of agricultural produce particularly affected during a critical period of the season.


  Government officials have emphasised that the current repair work forms part of a broader commitment to improve power infrastructure in the state. The collapse of the towers highlighted the vulnerability of the existing transmission network and the need for more resilient structures able to withstand extreme weather and other stresses. Officials noted that once the immediate repairs are finished, further assessments will be carried out to identify additional weak points along the line so that preventive maintenance can be strengthened.


  The return of power is expected to bring immediate relief to households that have been forced to rearrange daily routines around limited electricity windows. Students preparing for examinations will once again have consistent lighting for evening study. Small workshops and welding shops that depend on steady supply can resume fuller operations. Water pumping stations and other essential public services will also benefit from more reliable energy. In the longer term, improved power availability is expected to support industrial and commercial growth, making Kebbi more attractive for investment in agro-processing and other value-adding activities.


  KEDCO has appealed for patience during the final phase of the work, noting that safety protocols require thorough testing before the line is fully energised. The company has also reminded residents to report any remaining low voltage or irregular supply issues through official channels once restoration begins so that residual problems can be addressed quickly. Community leaders in the affected local government areas have been asked to help disseminate accurate information and discourage the spread of unverified claims about the timeline.


  The incident has renewed discussion about the overall state of electricity infrastructure in the northwest. Stakeholders point out that transmission and distribution networks across several states face similar risks from ageing equipment, vandalism and extreme weather. Strengthening these systems is seen as essential for economic recovery and for the success of broader development programmes. In Kebbi the state government has previously expressed support for investments that improve energy access, including renewable options that can complement the national grid.


  As the weekend approaches, attention remains focused on the technical teams still working on the line. If the schedule holds, thousands of residents will experience the return of steady electricity for the first time in weeks. The successful completion of the repairs will be measured not only by the restoration of supply but also by the ability of the system to remain stable under normal load conditions in the days and weeks that follow.


  Business associations in Birnin Kebbi have already begun preparing for the expected improvement. Some traders plan to restock refrigerated goods once they are confident the power will hold. Others intend to extend opening hours that had been cut short by generator costs. Health facility managers are looking forward to reduced reliance on diesel and the ability to run diagnostic equipment more consistently. Educational institutions hope that evening classes and computer laboratories can operate without interruption.


  The KEDCO Managing Director closed the briefing by reaffirming the commitment of the distribution company, the transmission authority and their partners to deliver reliable electricity. He stressed that the collaborative approach adopted for the current repairs will continue as further upgrades are planned. Residents were encouraged to continue practising energy efficiency even after supply returns so that the restored capacity can serve as many customers as possible without strain.


  For many households the coming weekend will mark the end of a difficult period marked by darkness, noise from generators and higher living costs. The restoration of power is more than a technical achievement. It represents a return to normal daily life for communities that have adapted as best they could while waiting for the damaged line to be repaired. If the work proceeds as announced, the lights will come back on across Birnin Kebbi, Argungu and Yauri, signalling the successful conclusion of a major repair effort and the beginning of a more stable phase for electricity supply in the state.


  Further updates are expected once the final tests are completed and the line is returned to commercial service. Until then, residents continue to monitor developments closely, hopeful that the assurance given at the Government House will be fulfilled within the stated timeframe. The coordinated response by the agencies involved has demonstrated the value of joint action in addressing critical infrastructure failures, and the outcome of this particular repair will be watched as an indicator of how future challenges of a similar nature can be managed.


  In the meantime, community support networks that emerged during the outage continue to operate. Neighbours sharing generator power, local groups organising bulk fuel purchases, and health workers adjusting schedules around available electricity have all helped to soften the impact. These informal arrangements are expected to wind down once the grid supply stabilises, but the experience has reinforced the importance of preparedness and mutual assistance in the face of service disruptions.


  The story of the collapsed towers and the subsequent repair effort will likely remain a reference point in discussions about power reliability in Kebbi for some time. It has illustrated both the fragility of existing infrastructure and the capacity of technical teams to mobilise when a major fault occurs. With the weekend restoration target now publicly stated, attention has shifted from diagnosis to delivery. Residents, businesses and public institutions are ready to reclaim the convenience and economic benefits that depend on consistent electricity. The coming days will determine whether the promise made at the Government House becomes the lived reality across the affected towns and villages.
