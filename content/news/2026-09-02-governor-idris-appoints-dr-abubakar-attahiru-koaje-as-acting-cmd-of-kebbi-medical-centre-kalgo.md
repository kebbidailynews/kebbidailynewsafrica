  Kebbi State Governor Dr. Nasir Idris has approved the appointment of Dr.
  Abubakar Attahiru Koaje as the Acting Chief Medical Director of the Kebbi
  Medical Centre located in Kalgo. The decision was formally announced by the
  Head of Service, Alhaji Sufyanu Garba Bena, and is widely viewed as a
  strategic step to consolidate gains in the state’s healthcare delivery system.


  According to the official announcement, Dr. Koaje, currently the most senior Director in the Medical Services Department of the state, has been entrusted with the leadership of the Kalgo facility. His previous role as Executive Secretary of the Primary Healthcare Development Management Agency equipped him with hands-on experience in coordinating primary care services, managing health workers, and implementing community-level health interventions across Kebbi’s 21 local government areas.


  The appointment is the latest in a series of administrative and infrastructural decisions aimed at improving the quality and accessibility of medical services. In recent years the state government has systematically upgraded facilities, relocated services to optimise space and equipment, and strengthened the human resource base of the health sector.


  Kebbi Medical Centre in Kalgo has undergone notable expansion. Several specialist departments that previously operated at the older Sir Yahaya Memorial Hospital in Birnin Kebbi were transferred to Kalgo to allow comprehensive remodelling of the capital’s historic hospital while ensuring continuity of care. The departments moved included Medicine, Surgery, Ophthalmology, Ear, Nose and Throat (ENT), Dental services, and Physiotherapy. Critical units such as Accident and Emergency, Maternity, Paediatrics, Intensive Care, Dialysis, Laboratory and X-ray services remained operational at Sir Yahaya during the transition.


  Officials at the time of the relocation explained that the temporary transfer was necessary to enable the physical upgrading of Sir Yahaya Memorial Hospital, established in 1952, so that it could meet contemporary standards of tertiary care. Patients were assured that medical records would be transferred and that shuttle buses and ambulances would support staff and emergency cases between the two sites. The Kalgo centre has since absorbed the additional workload and continues to function as a key secondary and emerging tertiary facility serving residents of Kalgo, surrounding communities and referrals from across the state.


  Dr. Koaje steps into leadership at a moment when the Kalgo Medical Centre is expected to play an even larger role in the state’s health architecture. The facility has benefited from equipment upgrades, solarisation projects, and improved mortuary services in recent months. These investments form part of a wider programme that has also seen renovations at general hospitals in Argungu, Yauri, Zuru and other locations, the procurement of ambulances, and the recruitment and regularisation of health workers.


  Governor Idris has repeatedly stated that healthcare remains a priority of his administration. In public remarks over the past two years he has linked improved hospital infrastructure, better conditions of service for doctors and nurses, and stronger primary healthcare systems to broader goals of reducing preventable deaths, supporting medical training, and retaining skilled personnel within the state. The elevation of an experienced administrator with a primary healthcare background is consistent with that stated philosophy.


  Colleagues familiar with Dr. Koaje’s earlier work at the Primary Healthcare Development Management Agency describe him as a methodical manager who prioritised outreach, immunisation coverage and the strengthening of primary health centres. His new assignment will require him to balance the clinical demands of a growing secondary facility with administrative responsibilities that include staff coordination, resource management, patient flow and collaboration with the Ministry of Health and tertiary training institutions.


  The Sir Yahaya Memorial Hospital remodelling project, which prompted the departmental transfers, remains a visible symbol of the government’s approach. Once the renovation is completed, the historic hospital is expected to resume full tertiary functions with modern equipment and improved capacity for specialist training. In the interim, the Kalgo centre has become an important complementary institution. Residents of Kalgo and neighbouring local government areas now have closer access to specialist outpatient and inpatient services that previously required travel into Birnin Kebbi.


  Reactions to the appointment have begun to emerge among health workers and civil society observers. Health professionals contacted for comment noted that continuity of leadership and institutional knowledge are essential when facilities are expanding. One senior medical officer in the state, speaking on condition of anonymity pending official circulars, said the choice of a director already familiar with both primary and secondary care systems should help bridge the two levels of service. Another practitioner observed that the Kalgo centre’s growth has created new administrative challenges that require experienced hands.


  On social media platforms, early commentary has largely focused on the broader health sector trajectory rather than the individual appointment. Posts circulating among Kebbi-based accounts and health advocacy groups have referenced the governor’s earlier decisions on salary structures for health workers, the regularisation of casual staff at medical facilities, and the provision of ambulances and diagnostic equipment. Some users welcomed the continued attention to hospital leadership, while others called for transparent performance metrics and regular public reporting on patient outcomes at upgraded centres. As of the time of writing, formal statements from professional bodies such as the Nigerian Medical Association state chapter or the Medical and Dental Consultants’ Association of Nigeria were still awaited.


  The appointment also occurs against the backdrop of ongoing efforts to strengthen medical education linkages. The Kebbi State Teaching Hospital framework and the training needs of medical students from Abdullahi Fodio University, Aliero, have influenced infrastructure decisions at Kalgo, including laboratory and mortuary upgrades. An acting CMD with a public health orientation is expected to support both service delivery and the teaching environment.


  Governor Idris’ administration has previously demonstrated willingness to make personnel changes when service delivery standards are judged inadequate. In one widely reported instance involving Argungu General Hospital, a chief medical director was reassigned following complaints about conditions at the facility. The current elevation of Dr. Koaje is presented by officials as a positive reinforcement of competence rather than a corrective measure.


  For patients and families who use the Kalgo centre, the practical implications will become clearer in the coming weeks. Continuity of specialist clinics, pharmacy supply, laboratory turnaround times and emergency response capacity will be the immediate tests of the new leadership. Longer-term expectations include further equipment deployment, staff motivation and clearer referral pathways between primary health centres, the Kalgo facility and tertiary institutions.


  Dr. Koaje’s background in primary healthcare may prove particularly relevant. Kebbi State, like much of northern Nigeria, continues to grapple with the dual burden of communicable diseases, maternal and child health challenges, and the rising incidence of non-communicable conditions. Strengthening the interface between community-level services and secondary hospitals remains a policy priority. An administrator who has coordinated primary care systems is well placed to advance that interface.


  The Head of Service’s announcement emphasised that the appointment reflects the governor’s commitment to efficient healthcare delivery. In the language of public service, “acting” status typically allows for performance assessment before a substantive confirmation. Stakeholders will therefore watch closely how the new leadership addresses existing bottlenecks at the Kalgo centre while the Sir Yahaya renovation proceeds.


  Beyond the individual appointment, the episode illustrates a pattern of institutional adaptation. Rather than attempting to modernise the ageing Sir Yahaya complex while maintaining full operations, the government chose temporary redistribution of services. That decision placed additional responsibility on Kalgo and now places corresponding responsibility on its new acting CMD. Success will be measured by the ability of both facilities to deliver reliable care during the transition period and by the quality of the upgraded Sir Yahaya hospital once it returns to full capacity.


  Health sector observers in the North-West have noted that several states are simultaneously investing in hospital infrastructure and leadership renewal. Kebbi’s approach has combined physical upgrades, departmental reorganisation, conditions-of-service improvements and selective appointments. The elevation of Dr. Abubakar Attahiru Koaje fits within that sequence.


  As the news of the appointment circulates, attention will shift from the announcement itself to implementation. Staff briefings, operational plans for the expanded department load, engagement with patient representatives and coordination with the Ministry of Health will occupy the early weeks of the new tenure. Public communication about clinic schedules, referral processes and any temporary adjustments will also be important for maintaining confidence among service users.


  Governor Idris has positioned healthcare as one of the pillars of his administration’s legacy. The appointment of an experienced director to lead a growing medical centre is consistent with that positioning. Whether the move delivers measurable improvements in patient experience, clinical outcomes and institutional efficiency will be the practical test in the months ahead.


  In the meantime, the people of Kalgo, Birnin Kebbi and the wider state have a new face at the helm of an important health institution. Dr. Abubakar Attahiru Koaje assumes the role of Acting Chief Medical Director of Kebbi Medical Centre with a mandate to sustain and advance the gains already recorded. The expectation, both official and public, is that the facility will continue to expand its capacity to serve the health needs of Kebbi residents while the parallel modernisation of Sir Yahaya Memorial Hospital reaches completion.


  The announcement marks another chapter in the ongoing reconfiguration of tertiary and secondary healthcare infrastructure in Kebbi State. It places professional experience, primary care insight and administrative seniority at the centre of a facility whose importance has grown substantially in recent years. For a state still working to overcome historical gaps in medical infrastructure and human resources, steady leadership at key institutions remains an essential ingredient of progress.
