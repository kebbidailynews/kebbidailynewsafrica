content: >-
  For many Nigerians, getting the right fashion pieces from the UK or other
  international markets can be frustrating. There are concerns about who to
  trust, how to pay, shipping, and whether the product will actually arrive as
  promised.




  For ClosetCater, that challenge became an opportunity.




  Through Closet Cater, an Enugu-based fashion business, Chisom is making international fashion more accessible to customers in Nigeria while also building a local fashion retail brand of her own.




  Closet Cater operates across two sides of the market. Customers can shop locally for fashion pieces for both men and women, while those looking for items from the UK can place personal shopping orders with Chisom. She sources the requested items directly from Britain and arranges delivery to Nigeria.




  The idea is simple: give Nigerians access to products they want without making the process unnecessarily complicated.




  One of the most interesting parts of Chisom's story is where she is building from.




  Lagos often dominates conversations about Nigerian fashion, retail and entrepreneurship. But businesses like Closet Cater are showing that ambitious brands can be built from other parts of the country.




  From Enugu, ClosetCater has created a business that connects Nigerian customers to an international market.




  That is a particularly Nigerian entrepreneurial story: spot a problem, find a way to solve it, and build trust one customer at a time.




  Closet Cater's Instagram presence reflects the way many modern Nigerian businesses are growing.




  With close to 3,000 followers and more than 4,000 posts, the account has become more than a social media page. It is effectively a digital showroom and business record.




  Customers can see products, orders, deliveries and feedback from people who have already bought from the business.




  Rather than relying entirely on polished advertising, Chisom has used consistency to build visibility. Every post, customer interaction and completed order contributes to the story of the brand.




  And in a market where trust is one of the biggest factors in online shopping, that consistency matters.




  What Chisom is building goes beyond selling clothes.




  The personal-shopping side of Closet Cater addresses a familiar problem for Nigerians: access.




  There are products Nigerians want but may not know how to source from abroad. Some people have relatives overseas who can help. Others do not. Some know where to shop but have no reliable way to get the items home.




  Closet Cater turns that arrangement into a service.




  Customers can tell Chisom what they are looking for, and she handles the sourcing process from the UK.




  That makes the business particularly relevant to Nigerians who want international fashion without having to navigate the entire process themselves.




  At the centre of the business is Chisom Nwebunu herself.




  Her story is a reminder that entrepreneurship is not always about raising millions of naira, opening a large showroom or attracting investors.




  Sometimes, it starts with identifying something people need and being willing to serve them consistently.




  Chisom has used her knowledge of fashion, access to the UK market and understanding of Nigerian customers to create a business model that works across borders.




  For aspiring entrepreneurs in the Southeast and across Nigeria, there is an important lesson here: you do not necessarily have to move to Lagos, London or another major commercial centre to build a business with international reach.




  A Nigerian Brand With International Reach




  Closet Cater is still growing, but its foundation is already clear: fashion, accessibility and customer trust.




  For Chisom, the opportunity is bigger than simply helping someone find a particular dress, shoe or fashion item.




  It is about creating a trusted bridge between Nigerian customers and international fashion markets while growing a Nigerian-owned brand in the process.




  From Nigeria to the UK, Chisom Nwebunu is showing what is possible when a simple business idea is matched with consistency and an understanding of what customers actually need.
